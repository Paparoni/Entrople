REFERENCES

  1. Shannon, C. E. (1948). A Mathematical Theory of Communication. Bell System Technical Journal, 27(3), 379–423. Source of the entropy formula H = −Σ pᵢ log₂ pᵢ used for every guess's expected information.
  2. Kullback, S. & Leibler, R. A. (1951). On Information and Sufficiency. Annals of Mathematical Statistics, 22(1), 79–86. Source of the DKL(P‖U) divergence used to score how uneven a guess's pattern split is.
  3. Aladaileh, T., Stephens, D., Alqaisi, M., & Wu, C. (2026). Solving Wordle Using Information Theory. Northeast Journal of Complex Systems, 8(1), Article 6. doi.org/10.63562/2577-8439.1146. Entropy-based guess selection applied specifically to Wordle, the basis for Entrople's "best guess" search.
  4. Mishra, N. (2024). Using Information Theory to Play Wordle as Optimally as Possible. NHSJS. nhsjs.com/?p=12299. Worked derivation of expected-information guess ranking that Entrople's entropy search follows.
  5. Healy, A. D. (2022). On Optimal Strategies for Wordle. alexhealy.net/papers/wordle.pdf. Source of the win-bonus correction Entrople's best-guess ranking uses: assigning the all-green outcome a value of −1 bit (rather than the 0 a singleton bucket would otherwise get) so a guess that can win outright is never just tied with an equally-splitting guess that can't.

HOW ENTROPLE WOULD SOLVE

WIN-BONUS ADJUSTED ROUTE

Set your actual guesses aside. This is the route Entrople's own win-bonus-adjusted entropy search takes on its own: every guess is whichever legal word carries the single highest adjusted-information score against the live candidate pool — raw Shannon entropy plus a small bonus for the chance the guess itself is the answer (see the win-bonus correction below) — narrowed guess by guess by the real feedback this answer produces. It is not plain entropy maximization: a guess that could win outright this turn is always favored over one that only splits the pool the same way without being able to win.
